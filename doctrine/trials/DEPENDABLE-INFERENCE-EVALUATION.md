## Decision

The four-object model can make a dependable derived conclusion of one class, and only that class held on both readings:

> A conditional already written in a record, applied to the question’s own antecedent, at the certainty already written on that record.

The candidate paragraph did not hold as a general license. On the one chain whose middle sentence was not written, both readings took the hop and reported no missing assumption.

**Recommendation: more trials required.**

`MODEL.md` is not amended. The sentence that would be worth trialing next is written below, and it is not installed. The project is not ready to leave doctrine research for a software prototype on the strength of this round.

## What held

The four objects already stored every premise these trials used. Project principles and the authority envelope, State facts and named uncertainties, Continuation questions, and Decision evidence were citable. Nothing in a held case required a fifth object.

Both readings, on the same frozen packet:

- Derived an explicit expiry or release when the conditional and the question’s antecedent were both present (Case A, F1, H1, and F6c question 5’s shelter conditional).
- Returned GAP when the record did not say what a deadline required (Case B) and when a delivery was only a user report (Case E).
- Kept Friday and Monday both open (Case D). Neither picked a day.
- Copied stipulated, user-reported, or conflicting certainty. Neither promoted a copied sentence into “the park will definitely” or “the delivery will arrive.”
- Answered “what happens if the reservation expires?” with the lost hall and nothing else (Case G).
- Refused to pay from a supported expiry (H2). Both said the envelope still governs.
- Stopped on deposits, parent transport, and next year’s priority (F3). Those facts are not in the fixture.
- Followed a two-step chain when each step’s sentence was written (F2).
- On Cedar Trail, refused to treat “the user does nothing” as “the shelter stays unconfirmed.”

F6c question 5 shows the sentence F6 would not spend. Asked whether the unresolved shelter persists, both readings applied the page rule that was already in v5. Asked whether the user does nothing, both returned GAP. Those are different questions. The records can answer the first by derivation. They do not contain the premise the second needs.

Recomputation was enough. The conclusions were produced from the current picture. They were not stored.

## What failed

### Case C — the unstated hop

The packet said the event requires the hall, the reservation expires if unpaid, the reservation is unpaid, and the deadline is today. It did not say that an expired reservation leaves the hall unavailable. That sentence was reserved for Case F, where it is written out.

The precommit said a level-1 expiry holds, a GAP that names the missing hop holds, and a silent hop fails.

R1 concluded that the hall-dependent outcome is threatened, called the inference level 2, and wrote “Missing assumptions: none,” while also writing that the record contains no premise that expiry leaves the hall unavailable. The gap was seen and not used.

R2 concluded the same threat, from “expired hall reservation” plus “the event requires the hall,” and also wrote “Missing assumptions: none.” It did not name the missing sentence.

Both FAIL Case C. They agree with each other. The failure is not a split between readers. The candidate rule’s phrase “every necessary premise” did not make either reader refuse a hop that felt lexical.

This does not, by the precommit, block the narrower class that held. It does block installing the candidate paragraph. A rule that both readers can satisfy on paper while taking the hop is not yet dependable.

No reading invented a substitute venue, ordered payment, or abandoned the project. The failure is narrower than a planning engine. It is still a conclusion the premises did not contain.

### What the depth limit did not test

F0, F1, and F2 behaved at the levels the fixtures wrote. F3 was both beyond level 2 and missing its facts. Both readings returned GAP. The fixture therefore does not show what a reader does with a fully written chain of three or four steps. “Further reasoning required” was not forced apart from “the premise is absent.” That confound stays open.

### Where the readings differed without failing

Recorded in `F6-cedar-trail-f6c.md`. The splits are how far “why today” joins the 17:00 line, how far `arose_from` is walked, and how many written conditionals an open “what follows” licenses. None of those splits contradicted a determined criterion. They were not averaged.

## Safe inference class

Consistently reliable in this round, on both readings:

1. **Level 0.** Quote the field. The deadline is 17:00 because the invoice says so.
2. **Level 1, explicit conditional.** The record says “if A, then B.” The question asks what follows if A. Conclude B. Certainty is the record’s certainty. The question’s A is a hypothetical, not a new fact.
3. **Level 2, only when each arrow is a written sentence.** Case F wrote “expiry means the hall is not reserved” and “the event requires the hall.” Both readings stayed on those sentences.
4. **GAP when the premise is absent,** including when the absent premise is the identity of two antecedents.
5. **Conflict left intact.**
6. **A report left as a report.**
7. **A narrow question left narrow.** Case G.
8. **A conclusion that does not act.** H2.

The safe class is recomputed. It does not need its own object.

## Unsafe inference class

Observed once, on both readings:

- **A plausible middle hop that was never written.** “The reservation expires” was treated as “the event in the hall is threatened,” without a sentence that expiry removes the hall. Case C.

Defined by the rule, and refused in this round rather than committed:

- An antecedent the question did not use and the record does not state. “The user does nothing” is not “the shelter stays unconfirmed.”
- A certainty upgrade from stipulated text, a user report, or a conflict into external fact.
- Choosing the fluent side of a disagreement.
- Work the question did not ask for: another venue, parents, transport, a budget, a task list.
- Authority inferred from a consequence. Expiry does not mean pay.
- A chain past the facts in the record. Deposits, transport, and next year’s priority.

Not tested cleanly:

- A long chain in which every step is written. The depth ceiling and the missing-premise rule were the same fixture.

## Disagreement

The packet was identical. Where the readings diverged, the cause was an underspecified question (“why today,” “the present condition,” “what follows”), not two different rules and not a contradicted determined case.

Where they agreed on Case C, the cause was the candidate rule. “Necessary premise” did not specify that a lexical hop still needs its own sentence. That is a defect in the rule text. It is shared. It is not cured by averaging, and it is not cured by preferring the more fluent reading. There was no more fluent reading. They matched.

Cross-family stability is not established. The two requests named different model parameters. Both running models are `unknown`. Agreement under unverified identity is evidence about these two passes. It is not yet a routing result. No router is designed here.

---

## Model A and Model B

**Model A.** State keeps facts and uncertainties. A consequence is derived when a question supplies the antecedent the record already uses.

**Model B.** An uncertainty would store a pointer, of the form: unresolved until a time, then a named consequence.

What the readings showed:

- Question 5’s shelter conditional was recalculated from v5 and D2. Both readers found it. Model A answered that question without a new field.
- Question 6 stayed GAP. A pointer on U-shelter would answer “if this uncertainty remains until 17:00.” It would not answer “if the user does nothing,” unless the pointer silently renamed one as the other. That rename is the premise the readings refused. Storing it would make the record less dependable than the GAP.
- The release sentence is already in the current picture. A pointer would be a second copy. A later page, if one were ever actually read, would have to update the picture and the pointer or they would disagree. The trial did not show invalidation logic that would be safer than rereading the picture.
- Case C is the warning against Model B. Both readers produced a conclusion the precommit called unpremised. Persisting that conclusion would have made the hop a fact. Recomputation at least left the error in the reading, where this audit could see it.
- Case F shows the other side. When the middle sentence is stored as an ordinary fact, the level-2 conclusion can be rebuilt. The conclusion itself does not need a home.
- “Why today?” was answerable from C3’s time. R1 could also cite the page time. R2 did not. The split is not a missing field. F6 already declined to add one. F6c does not reverse that.

Model A is the more dependable of the two for the class that held. Model B is not adopted. No `if_unresolved_until` field. No `consequence` field.

## Open questions

- Will a tighter sentence, one that calls an unwritten hop a GAP even when the words feel obvious, actually be followed? The sentence below was not the rule these readers saw. It is untested.
- Does “the event requires the hall” plus “an expired reservation is not a reservation of the hall” already mean “the event cannot take place there”? The precommit scored that Case F conclusion as a hold. The last arrow is only as strong as that choice.
- When “what follows” matches two written conditionals, must the reading state both? R1 did. R2 stated the one with a clock. The no-expand rule kept both of them off the task list. It did not say how many true conditionals are one answer.
- What does a reader do with three written steps? This round does not say.
- The page was stipulated. Nothing here is a real park, a second project, joint authority, or a six-month resume.
- Two passes are not a test of model routing.

## The sentence that is not installed

A later trial could test this wording. It is narrower than the candidate paragraph. It is not v0.4. It is not in `MODEL.md`.

> A conclusion may be derived only when every premise is a persisted field, or is the hypothetical antecedent of the question being asked, and the inference is either one step or two steps whose sentences are each written in the record.
>
> The conclusion uses the weakest certainty those premises already have.
>
> If the question’s antecedent is not the record’s antecedent, the result is GAP.
>
> If the premises conflict, the conflict remains.
>
> If a further step has no written sentence, the result is GAP, including when the step sounds obvious.
>
> Beyond two written steps, stop.
>
> A derived conclusion does not authorize Act, and it does not authorize additional work.

Installing it now would repeat the mistake Case C exposed: treating a sentence as dependable because it describes the behavior we wanted, before a reading has had to obey it.

---

## Recommendation

**More trials required.**

The four-object model is sufficient for the records these cases needed. It is not insufficient. A small field is not justified. No doctrine change is made. The candidate inference rule is not dependable enough to install.

The next trial, if one is authorized, is a reread of a Case C fixture under the tighter sentence, plus one chain of three written steps. Until that reading exists, the operating doctrine stays v0.3, and the honest end of the cycle is Wait.
